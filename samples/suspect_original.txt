Quantum computing is an emerging technology that harnesses the principles of
quantum mechanics to perform computations that would be infeasible for
classical computers. Unlike traditional bits that exist as either 0 or 1,
quantum bits or qubits can exist in a superposition of both states simultaneously.

This property, combined with quantum entanglement and interference, allows
quantum computers to process vast amounts of information in parallel. Quantum
algorithms such as Shor's algorithm for integer factorisation and Grover's
algorithm for database search offer exponential speedups over classical approaches.

Quantum computing holds promise for applications in cryptography, drug discovery,
materials science, and optimisation problems. Companies like IBM, Google, and
Microsoft are racing to build practical quantum computers with sufficient qubits
and low error rates to achieve quantum advantage.
